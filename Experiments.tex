\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments answer four questions. \textbf{RQ1}: inside one RL system and under one recipe, does CIS improve held-out accuracy and training stability over the cap, the gate, and the exact ratio? \textbf{RQ2}: which elements of the operator matter, and in which direction? \textbf{RQ3}: how sensitive is CIS to its two hyperparameters, and can they be set from measurements rather than by search? \textbf{RQ4}: why does CIS work? Section~\ref{sec:experiments-setup} describes the shared setup; each subsequent subsection answers one question. Implementation details, the diagnostics behind the two-channel model, and additional tables and figures are in Appendix~\ref{app:experimental}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:experiments-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Mismatch measurement.}\label{sec:exp_measurement}
We measure $(k_t,p_t)$ on 12.3M tokens of Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat and 12.4M tokens of a dense control (Qwen1.5-14B-Chat): vLLM samples eight responses per DAPO-Math prompt at temperature one and records each sampled token's log-probability at the sampling step, and the training engine rescores the same sequences at the same parameters. Controls, the paired stale-snapshot measurement behind Figure~\ref{fig:two_channel}, and training-time logging are in Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-diagnostics}.

\noindent\textbf{Training.}
We train Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat with AReaL on the GSM8K training set (SGLang rollouts, FSDP training, both bf16) and report greedy exact-match accuracy on the 1{,}319 held-out test problems (standard error about $1.3$ points per arm), with three seeds per method and one further run per method with the KV cache quantized to FP8. All arms share one recipe and code path (Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-implementation}) and differ only in the operator: no correction, the exact ratio, TIS (cap $2$), IcePop (mask $[0.5,5]$), the binary-KL mask of AReaL (KPop, threshold $2$), and CIS (\Cref{alg:cis}, $\lambda_+=2.3$, $\kappa=5\times10^{-3}$).

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares six operators applied to the same pair $(k_t,p_t)$ in the same code; per-seed values and training statistics are in Table~\ref{tab:main_seeds} of the appendix. Correcting the mismatch in any bounded way is the largest effect: the corrected runs gain between $3.3$ and $8.7$ points over the uncorrected loss, whose training reward stalls at $0.73$ while every corrected run exceeds $0.82$. Among the corrections, CIS has the highest mean, the smallest spread across seeds, and the highest worst seed ($63.23$ against $62.70$ for IcePop and $62.40$ for TIS), and it also has the highest training reward. The three leading methods lie within one standard error of one another, so we do not claim a significant ordering among them on this task and model. The exact ratio has a healthy reward curve on every seed but a held-out spread of $9.1$ points, and on one seed it falls to the level of no correction while its training reward stays close to the other seeds. The KL mask intervenes on about a fifth of all tokens; this rate is a numerical property of the shipped implementation, in which a token whose probability saturates at one on either engine is masked (Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-results}), and the numerically corrected variant recovers two points but stays below the cap, the gate, and the band. No operator adds a forward or backward pass, and all arms take $31$ to $34$ seconds per optimizer step. Held-out accuracy and training reward disagree in several places in Table~\ref{tab:main}; whenever they disagree, the training reward is the quantity that is blind to the difference. Training curves, held-out accuracy at every epoch checkpoint, gradient norms, and intervention rates over training are in Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-dynamics}; on the epoch checkpoints CIS leads at every epoch and is the only method whose accuracy does not dip.

\noindent\textbf{Slope and floor.}
CIS has two hyperparameters, and Table~\ref{tab:tuning} (left) sweeps each with the other fixed. Held-out accuracy is flat on the plateau $\lambda_+\in\{0.9,1.6,2.3\}$, with $64.44$, $64.67$, and $64.67$, and declines slightly at $3.1$, so the slope is insensitive within a factor of two and a half. The value $0.9$ is not a grid point but the output of the compilation $\lambda_+=c\tau^\star$ of \Cref{thm:cis_informal}: with the base-model constant $c=0.155$, the measured tail of $F_Z$, and $\hat\epsilon=1.76\%$, the equalizer condition has the root $\tau^\star=5.7$ (Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-compile}), so the compiled slope lands on the plateau without a training run. The floor behaves differently. Without it the band collapses at high confidence onto tokens whose $|\log k_t|$ is at the storage resolution of the log-probabilities and training diverges at step 79; raising it to $\kappa=0.1$, which places $58\%$ of tokens under a constant cap, costs $3.3$ points on three seeds while leaving the training reward unchanged. The floor is the storage resolution, $5\times10^{-3}$, and it is read off the logs rather than tuned (Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-ablation}).

\noindent\textbf{A changed serving configuration.}
The slope is proportional to the scale constant $c$, and $c$ is a property of the model and of the engine configuration. Table~\ref{tab:tuning} (right) quantizes the SGLang KV cache to FP8, which raises the training-time scale constant, measured on tokens generated and scored at the same parameter version, from $0.60$ to $1.75$, and raises the fraction of such tokens truncated by the bf16 slope from $1.8\%$ to $5.2\%$. With the bf16 slope CIS loses $4.6$ points. Rescaling the slope by the measured ratio $2.9$, to $\lambda_+=6.7$, recovers $2.9$ of these points and brings CIS within one standard error of the best baseline, whereas an intermediate slope of $4.0$ recovers nothing. The baselines behave differently: the ratio mask of IcePop is nearly unaffected by quantization, the KL mask falls below the untrained model, which is consistent with its saturation artifact becoming more frequent under quantization, and the uncorrected run collapses in the middle of training. The comparison is single-seed and we report it as such. RQ3 is answered as follows: the slope is insensitive within a wide plateau and the compiled value lies on it, the floor is fixed by the storage resolution, and when the serving configuration changes the slope should be recompiled from the measured $c$ of that configuration rather than carried over.
