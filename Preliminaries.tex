\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Training--inference mismatch}
\label{sec:prelim_mismatch}

In current RL post-training frameworks, rollouts are generated by an inference
engine (vLLM, SGLang) while gradients are computed by a training engine (FSDP,
Megatron). The two load the same parameters $\theta$, yet differing kernel
implementations, reduction orders, and precisions make them assign different
probabilities to the same token,
$\pi_{\mathrm{train}}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t};\theta)\ne
\pi_{\mathrm{infer}}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t};\theta)$,
so that nominally on-policy training is in fact off-policy. The problem is more
severe on mixture-of-experts models, where routing is a discrete top-$k$
decision and a small numerical perturbation can change which experts are
activated.

\subsection{The objective with a ratio operator}
\label{sec:prelim_operator}

Corrections of the mismatch can be written uniformly as a ratio operator
$\Mclass$ multiplying the token-level GRPO objective,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J(\theta)
=
\E_{x\sim\mathcal D,\;\{y_i\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\mathrm{infer}}(\cdot\mid x;\theta_{\mathrm{old}})}
\left[
\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\abs{y_i}}\sum_{t=1}^{\abs{y_i}}
\Mclass\big(k_{i,t}\big)\,
\min\Big(
r_{i,t}\hat A_{i,t},\;
\operatorname{clip}\big(r_{i,t}\big)\hat A_{i,t}
\Big)
\right],
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where
$r_{i,t}=\pi_{\mathrm{train}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t};\theta)\big/
\pi_{\mathrm{train}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t};\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$
and
$k_{i,t}=\pi_{\mathrm{train}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t};\theta_{\mathrm{old}})\big/
\pi_{\mathrm{infer}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t};\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$,
with $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$ the parameter snapshot at rollout time and
$\hat A_{i,t}$ the group-normalized advantage. The ratio $r_{i,t}$ is handled by
the clip of standard PPO; $k_{i,t}$ is constrained by the operator $\Mclass$
defined in this work.
\subsection{Operator design as an estimation problem}
\label{sec:prelim_estimation}

A gradient update uses $n$ tokens (all tokens of all trajectories in the
batch). Write the payload of the $j$-th token as
$\ell'_j=\nabla_\theta\min\big(r_j\hat A_j,\operatorname{clip}(r_j)\hat A_j\big)$,
the gradient of the surrogate term in \eqref{eq:objective}. Since
$\Mclass(k_j)$ does not depend on $\theta$, the gradient of the objective and
its $n$-sample estimator are
$\nabla_\theta\mathcal J=\E[\Mclass(k)\,\ell']$ and
$\widehat G_{\Mclass}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Mclass(k_j)\,\ell'_j$, where
the expectation is over the rollout distribution, $x\sim\mathcal D$ and
$y\sim\pi_{\mathrm{infer}}(\cdot\mid x;\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$. We restrict the
operator to the shrinkage class
$\Mdown:=\{\Mclass:\ 0<\Mclass(k)\le k\}$, which may attenuate a ratio but
never amplify it. What $\widehat G_{\Mclass}$ should approximate, and at what
cost, depends on what $k$ is made of; we therefore measure it first.
