\section{The CIS Framework}
\label{sec:cis}
\subsection{A logit-displacement model of the mismatch}
\label{sec:logit_model}

\begin{wrapfigure}[11]{r}{0.5\textwidth}
\vspace{-1.2\baselineskip}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{figures/fig1_eps_delta.pdf}
\vspace{-1.4\baselineskip}
\caption{(a) $\varepsilon_t$ on the trained MoE (log scale): the bulk $F_0$ and
the residual $G_{p_t}$ add up to the total. (b) Per-logit perturbation
$\delta_j$ on the base MoE and a dense control.}
\label{fig:mismatch}
\end{wrapfigure}

We systematically measure, on large-scale rollouts, the probabilities that the
two engines assign to the same sampled token; experimental details are given in
\Cref{sec:exp_measurement}. Write $h_t=(x,y_{<t})$ for the current prefix,
$p_t=\pi_{\mathrm{train}}(y_t\mid h_t;\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$ and
$q_t=\pi_{\mathrm{infer}}(y_t\mid h_t;\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$, where $y_t$ is
the token sampled by the inference engine, and drop the trajectory index $i$ in
what follows. The measurements show that the discrepancy between the two engines
has a stable bulk structure in the log-odds coordinate together with a sparse
residual component, which we model as follows.

\begin{definition}[Logit-displacement model]
\label{def:logit_displacement}
For a token sampled by the inference engine,
\begin{equation}
\logit q_t=\logit p_t-\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
\varepsilon_t\mid p_t\;\sim\;(1-\eta)\,F_0+\eta\,G_{p_t},
\label{eq:logit_displacement}
\end{equation}
where the bulk law $F_0$ does not depend on $p_t$ and satisfies
$\E_{F_0}[e^{2\varepsilon}]<\infty$, the residual law $G_{p_t}$ is arbitrary
and may depend on $p_t$, and $\eta\ll1$.
\end{definition}

Here $\varepsilon_t$ is the log-odds displacement of the inference engine
relative to the training engine on the current token. For the bounds below, we
additionally assume that the first exponential moment is finite and write
$\mu:=\E[e^{\varepsilon_t}]<\infty$. \Cref{def:logit_displacement} is a
conditional form of Huber's contamination model~\citep{huber1964robust}: most
displacements follow a bulk law that does not depend on the confidence, while a
small fraction follows an arbitrary, possibly confidence-dependent law.
\Cref{fig:mismatch}(a) shows the measured distribution: the bulk accounts for
almost all tokens, while the residual covers only a few tokens but produces a
long tail.

\paragraph{Where the structure comes from.}
Write $\mathbf z\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$ for the pre-softmax logit vector of the
training engine at the prefix $h_t$, and $\mathbf z+\boldsymbol\delta$ for that
of the inference engine, so that
\begin{equation}
p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z)_{y_t},
\qquad
q_t=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z+\boldsymbol\delta)_{y_t},
\label{eq:engine_logits}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$ is the per-logit perturbation between
the two engines. Write $w_j:=e^{z_j}\big/\sum_{j'\ne y_t}e^{z_{j'}}$ for
$j\ne y_t$. Taking the logit of both sides of \eqref{eq:engine_logits} and
subtracting gives
\begin{equation}
\logit p_t-\logit q_t
\;=\;
-\delta_{y_t}+\log\sum_{j\ne y_t} w_j\,e^{\delta_j}
\;=:\;
\varepsilon_t .
\label{eq:eps_identity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:eps_identity} answers two questions. First, why the log-odds
coordinate: the softmax normalization cancels when forming odds, so the
discrepancy between the two engines is an additive displacement in this
coordinate. Second, why the displacement consists of a bulk and a residual:
\eqref{eq:eps_identity} expresses $\varepsilon_t$ as a function of the logit
perturbation $\boldsymbol\delta$, and measuring $\boldsymbol\delta$ directly
(\Cref{fig:mismatch}(b)) shows that the two components are already present at
the logit level. On the dense control, $\boldsymbol\delta$ is concentrated near
zero and bounded; on the MoE, the body is likewise concentrated near zero but
carries a heavy tail. The former is consistent with bf16 rounding error; the
latter appears mainly on the MoE, where routing disagreement between the engines
is an important contributor (\Cref{app:diagnostics}).

\paragraph{From displacement to ratio.}
Solving \eqref{eq:logit_displacement} for the mismatch ratio $k_t:=p_t/q_t$
gives
\begin{equation}
k_t=p_t+(1-p_t)\,e^{\varepsilon_t},
\qquad
k_t-1=(1-p_t)\big(e^{\varepsilon_t}-1\big).
\label{eq:k_geometry}
\end{equation}
Given $p_t$, the ratio is a deterministic, increasing function of
$\varepsilon_t$: the mismatch enters the importance weight only through the
displacement factor $e^{\varepsilon_t}$, rescaled by $1-p_t$. The same
displacement therefore moves the ratio by an amount of order one on a
low-confidence token, but only by an amount of order $1-p_t$ on a
high-confidence token.

\subsection{Why exact correction is unstable}
\label{sec:why_correction}

A gradient update uses $n$ tokens, indexed by $j$. Write the payload of the
$j$-th token as
$\ell'_j=\nabla_\theta\min\big(\rho_j\hat A_j,\operatorname{clip}(\rho_j)\hat A_j\big)$;
the mismatch ratio $k_j$ is the importance sampling factor that multiplies it.
Since $k_j$ does not depend on $\theta$, the gradient of the token-level
surrogate objective and its estimator are
\begin{equation*}
G_0:=\nabla_\theta\mathcal J=\E[k\,\ell'],
\qquad
\widehat G:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_j\,\ell'_j ,
\end{equation*}
where the expectation is over the prompt, the rollout, and the numerical
discrepancy between the two engines on each token. $G_0$ corrects the
conditional distribution of each token given its prefix, not the distribution
of the prefix itself. We measure the quality of the estimate by the
mean-squared error $\E\big\|\widehat G-G_0\big\|_2^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Informal]
\label{thm:exact_risk}
Assume $\|\ell'_t\|_2\le b$ and that the contributions of different tokens are
independent (for correlated contributions, replace $n$ by $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$;
see \Cref{app:proofs}). Then
\begin{equation}
\E\big\|\widehat G-G_0\big\|_2^2
\;\le\;
\frac{b^2}{n}\Big(1+2\mu+\E\big[e^{2\varepsilon_t}\big]\Big).
\label{eq:exact_risk}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\Cref{fig:mgf} shows $\E[e^{\alpha\varepsilon_t}]$ on the measured
displacements (\Cref{app:mgf}): it is only $7.07$ at $\alpha=1$ but reaches
$9.2\times10^{6}$ at $\alpha=2$, so the bound \eqref{eq:exact_risk} is dominated
by the second moment. This second moment comes almost entirely from a few
tokens in the upper tail: they make up $0.66\%$ of all tokens but contribute
$99.999\%$ of it. Exact correction is therefore unbiased but has an
uncontrolled variance, and some bias must be accepted in exchange for a bounded
one.

Existing methods do so by applying a fixed threshold directly to $k_t$: TIS
truncates it to $\min\{k_t,C\}$ \citep{cite}, IcePop masks tokens with
$k_t\notin[a,b]$ \citep{cite}, and KPop masks by a fixed KL threshold
\citep{cite}. All of them act on the ratio $k_t$ rather than on the source of
the problem, $\varepsilon_t$. By \eqref{eq:k_geometry}, $k_t$ is
$\varepsilon_t$ rescaled by $1-p_t$, so the same threshold on $k_t$ constrains
$\varepsilon_t$ differently at different confidence levels. The next section
places the threshold on $\varepsilon_t$ directly.

\subsection{Correction in the displacement coordinate}
\label{sec:optimal_operator}

By \eqref{eq:k_geometry}, given $p_t$ the mismatch enters the ratio only through
$e^{\varepsilon_t}$, and by \Cref{sec:why_correction} the variance explosion
comes from the upper tail of $e^{\varepsilon_t}$. We therefore bound the
displacement directly:
\begin{equation}
e^{\varepsilon_t}\le1+\lambda,\qquad \lambda\ge0 .
\label{eq:disp_threshold}
\end{equation}
All tokens share the same $\lambda$ because, by \Cref{def:logit_displacement},
the normal range of the displacement is the same at every confidence level, so
$e^{\varepsilon_t}>1+\lambda$ signals the same degree of abnormality on any
token. Truncating $e^{\varepsilon_t}$ at $1+\lambda$ and mapping back through
\eqref{eq:k_geometry} gives
\begin{equation}
f_{\lambda}(k_t,p_t)
\;=\;
p_t+(1-p_t)\min\big\{e^{\varepsilon_t},\,1+\lambda\big\}
\;=\;
\min\big\{k_t,\;1+\lambda(1-p_t)\big\}.
\label{eq:cis_operator}
\end{equation}
We call \eqref{eq:cis_operator} \emph{calibrated importance sampling} (CIS).
The factor $1-p_t$ in the cap is not a design choice: it is the factor by which
\eqref{eq:k_geometry} maps displacements to ratios.

\begin{theorem}[Informal]
\label{thm:cis_risk}
Assume $\|\ell'_t\|_2\le b$ and that the contributions of different tokens are
independent. Then for any finite $\lambda\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\E\big\|\widehat G_{f_{\lambda}}-G_0\big\|_2^2
\;\le\;
b^2\Big(\E\big[(e^{\varepsilon_t}-1-\lambda)_+\big]\Big)^2
+\frac{b^2}{n}\Big(1+2\mu+\E\big[\min(e^{\varepsilon_t},1+\lambda)^2\big]\Big).
\label{eq:cis_risk}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Compared with \eqref{eq:exact_risk}, the only change is that the second moment
$\E[e^{2\varepsilon_t}]$ is replaced by
$\E[\min(e^{\varepsilon_t},1+\lambda)^2]\le(1+\lambda)^2$. However heavy the
tail, the second moment of $9.2\times10^{6}$ in \Cref{fig:mgf} is brought below
$(1+\lambda)^2$. The price is a bias from the truncated excess, which is
controlled by the first moment. $\lambda$ trades off the two: a smaller
$\lambda$ gives a smaller variance and a larger bias, and $\lambda\to\infty$
recovers exact correction.

\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.5\textwidth}
\vspace{-\baselineskip}
\begin{minipage}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Calibrated importance sampling (CIS)}
\label{alg:cis}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\ell^{\mathrm{train}}_j$, $\ell^{\mathrm{infer}}_j$, $\ell'_j$ for
the $n$ tokens of the batch; threshold $\lambda$; floor $\kappa$
\For{$j=1,\dots,n$}
  \State $p_j\gets\exp(\ell^{\mathrm{train}}_j)$,\quad
  $k_j\gets\exp(\ell^{\mathrm{train}}_j-\ell^{\mathrm{infer}}_j)$
  \State $\varphi_j\gets\max(1-p_j,\,\kappa)$
  \State $f_j\gets\min\big\{k_j,\;1+\lambda\varphi_j\big\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\widehat G_{\mathrm{CIS}}
=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{stopgrad}(f_j)\,\ell'_j$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\end{minipage}
\end{wrapfigure}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
We treat $\lambda$ as a hyperparameter; \Cref{sec:exp_hyperparameters} shows
that the result is insensitive to it over a wide range. Applying
\eqref{eq:cis_operator} in practice raises one issue: as $1-p_t$ becomes very
small, the cap $1+\lambda(1-p_t)$ approaches one and becomes narrower than the
storage resolution of the log-probabilities. The truncated tokens then have
$|\log k_t|$ at the level of that resolution, so the truncation acts on
rounding error rather than on the mismatch; without a remedy, training diverges
at step 79 (\Cref{app:ablations}). We therefore floor $1-p_t$ at the storage
resolution $\kappa=5\times10^{-3}$, replacing it by
$\varphi_t=\max(1-p_t,\kappa)$, which gives \Cref{alg:cis}. The correction adds
one elementwise pass and no forward or backward computation. The
inference-side log-probability must be the raw value recorded at the sampling
step, and the weight must be detached.
