\title{Uncertainty-Set Geometry Determines the Algorithm:\\
A Uniform-Risk Account of Training--Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

% \iclrfinalcopy

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Modern reinforcement learning for large language models uses one engine to generate rollouts and another to compute gradients. Even at synchronized parameters, the two engines can assign different probabilities to the same token; in mixture-of-experts models, a router flip can also assign the gradient to experts that did not generate the token. Existing remedies apply different operations to the resulting engine ratio: truncated importance sampling caps it, whereas IcePop zeroes ratios outside a fixed band. We formulate two observation-law classes and ask which weight map minimizes a bias constant uniformly over each class. Following the closed-form characterization and matching hard-instance organization used in distributionally robust offline RL, we prove two correspondence theorems. For bounded log-density ratios, capping is calibrated, is the unique projection under a fixed influence budget for every admissible ratio law, and loses its uniform guarantee once an essential escape component leaves every finite band. For a localized corrupted channel, unrestricted support makes capping and gating statistically nonidentifiable, whereas localization yields an exact uniform-risk solution: the identity outside the escape set and a clean-tail-quantile cap inside it. The IcePop gate is the sharp zero-cap phase, selected if and only if positive clean escape mass is no larger than contamination odds. The upper guarantees and the attaining members match exactly, so the constants cannot be improved within the stated classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern language-model RL is implemented by two software stacks. An inference engine such as vLLM or SGLang generates rollouts, while a training engine such as Megatron or FSDP recomputes log probabilities and backpropagates gradients. Different kernels, parallelism layouts, reduction orders, precisions, and decode paths make these stacks numerically distinct even when they load the same parameters \citep{yao2025tis,he2025nondeterminism,qi2025fp16}. The discrepancy is especially consequential in mixture-of-experts models. A small numerical perturbation can change a discrete top-$k$ routing decision, so the training path may traverse experts that did not generate the sampled token \citep{ling2025ring,ma2025r3}. The mismatch can then affect both the token probability and the conditional mean of the gradient payload.

Practice has produced two different repairs. Truncated importance sampling (TIS) caps the engine ratio \citep{ionides2008truncated,yao2025tis}; IcePop zeroes every ratio outside a fixed interval \citep{ling2025ring}. Both require choices outside the underlying RL objective. This leaves three questions unanswered: why should a large ratio be capped rather than removed, when should it instead be removed, and what uncertainty assumptions make either operation uniformly valid?

\paragraph{Our view.}
An uncertainty class is not merely a regularizer around a fixed algorithm. Together with a decision criterion, it selects the token-level operator and determines the boundary of its guarantee. A bounded-ratio class treats every payload as valid but potentially influential, leading to a projection onto an influence budget. A localized corrupted channel allows a small fraction of payload means to be unknown without imposing a zero-mean condition, leading to a clean-fidelity versus corrupted-exposure tradeoff. The cap and the gate are therefore solutions to different statistical problems rather than interchangeable heuristics.

Our contributions are threefold.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize geometry--operator correspondence through two requirements: an operator must minimize a bias constant uniformly over a stated observation-law class, and a matching hard instance must characterize the boundary beyond which its guarantee fails. This follows the closed-form-plus-lower-bound proof organization of \citet{shi2023distributionally}, while changing the statistical object from a robust Bellman functional to a ratio-weighted gradient estimator.
    \item We prove a complete density-band correspondence. A cap covering the band is exactly unbiased; under a fixed influence budget it is the unique pointwise projection for every admissible ratio law; and an essential escape component places the observation law outside every finite band and creates a bias floor of at least $\eps B e^{\sigma}$ for every band-faithful cap.
    \item We prove a complete localized-corruption correspondence. Without localization, the cap-versus-gate choice is not identifiable from the class. With localization, the uniform-risk optimal rule is the identity outside the escape set and $\min\{k,m^{\star}\}$ inside it. Hard zeroing is optimal if and only if positive clean escape mass is at most $\eps/(1-\eps)$, and an attaining-member construction matches the uniform constant for every measurable operator.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Notation.}
The differentiable training policy is $p_{\theta}$ and the rollout policy is $q_{\theta}$. At synchronized parameters $\theta_0$, the engine ratio is $K=p_{\theta_0}/q_{\theta_0}$, with $k$ denoting a realized value. The full importance ratio factors as $w=rK$, where $r=p_{\theta}/p_{\theta_0}$ is parameter-space drift. We write $P_K$ for a clean ratio law, $P_0$ for the clean law in the localized model, $\Lset$ for a reachable escape set, and $\eps$ for the corrupted-channel mass. The payload class is $\Xclass_B=\{x:\norm{x(k)}\le B\}$, the clean tail is $T(m)=P_0(K>m,K\in\Lset)$, and $\pi_{\Lset}=P_0(K\in\Lset,K>0)$.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Training--inference mismatch in language-model RL.}
Current post-training pipelines split rollout generation and gradient computation across two engines. Inference systems such as vLLM and SGLang optimize throughput with paged attention, continuous batching, and fused kernels \citep{kwon2023vllm,zheng2024sglang}, whereas training systems such as FSDP or Megatron recompute log-probabilities with different kernels, reduction orders, and parallel layouts. The two realizations disagree even at identical parameters, and the disagreement depends on batch composition and decode path rather than being a fixed rounding offset \citep{he2025nondeterminism}. \citet{yao2025tis} show that this discrepancy turns nominally on-policy training into off-policy training and propose truncated importance sampling on the engine ratio. The Ring-1T report introduces IcePop, which zeroes tokens whose ratio leaves a fixed interval, and documents compounding mismatch over long runs together with sensitivity to the interval \citep{ling2025ring}. Systems-level remedies act on the source: FP16 arithmetic reduces the numerical discrepancy \citep{qi2025fp16}, Rollout Routing Replay reuses inference-time expert routes on the training side \citep{ma2025r3}, and DRPO replaces hard masking with a smooth divergence penalty \citep{yao2026drpo}. GSPO moves the correction unit from tokens to sequences \citep{zheng2025gspo}. Asynchronous systems such as AReaL add a second source of displacement, because a rollout may be scored by a parameter snapshot that is several versions newer than the snapshot which generated it \citep{fu2025areal}. We do not propose a new systems control. We ask which token-level operator is justified once the mismatch has been measured, and we evaluate the resulting operator against the cap and the gate inside one training system.

\paragraph{Mixture-of-experts routing.}
Sparse expert models route each token to a top-$k$ subset of experts through a learned gate \citep{fedus2022switch}. The routing decision is discrete, so a small perturbation of the gate logits can change which experts process a token, and the training engine may then score a token on experts that did not generate it \citep{ma2025r3,ling2025ring}. Our measurements treat this event as one source of a residual component of the mismatch, and we quantify how strongly it is enriched among large deviations.

\paragraph{Importance weighting and truncation in off-policy RL.}
Truncated importance sampling trades a controlled bias for bounded variance \citep{ionides2008truncated}. In deep RL the same device appears as the truncated traces of Retrace and the clipped weights of V-trace, both of which cap per-step ratios at a constant \citep{munos2016retrace,espeholt2018impala}. PPO and its group-relative variant GRPO handle parameter drift with a clipped surrogate rather than with an importance weight \citep{schulman2017ppo,shao2024deepseekmath}, and DAPO decouples the two clip ranges \citep{yu2025dapo}. All of these operators use a threshold which does not depend on the token. The operator studied here differs in that its threshold follows the measured conditional width of the mismatch.

\paragraph{Robust statistics and robust RL.}
Huber's contamination model and the associated minimax theory formalize a small fraction of arbitrary deviations \citep{huber1964robust,huber1965robust,huber1973minimax}, and CVaR admits a density-bounded dual representation \citep{rockafellar2000cvar}. In reinforcement learning, value clipping can be the exact image of a robust Bellman problem under total-variation geometry \citep{liu2026clipped}, and a line of work relates robustness to regularization and to divergence-structured robust MDPs \citep{derman2021twice,yang2023rmdp,zhang2024soft,tang2025f}. \citet{shi2023distributionally} organize KL-robust offline RL around an explicit nominal functional and a matching hard instance. We adopt the same discipline of deriving the exact population operator first and then exhibiting the class members which attain its constant. Classical robust testing distinguishes censoring from trimming, but it does not model a gradient payload whose computational path can be wrong. We encode that possibility as an unknown corrupted payload mean without a zero-mean assumption.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Setup and notation}

Let $x$ denote a prompt and $\tau=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)$ a trajectory, with prefix
$h_t=(x,y_{<t})$, so that any autoregressive policy factorizes as
$\pi(\tau\mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\pi(y_t\mid h_t)$. Modern RL post-training
pipelines maintain \emph{two numerical realizations} of the rollout policy: an
inference engine optimized for throughput, which generates the data, and a
training engine, which scores it and carries gradients. We write
$\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}_{\theta}$ and $\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta}$ for the two
realizations of the same parameter vector $\theta$, and
$\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$ for the parameter snapshot at rollout time.

Three per-token scalars organize everything that follows. For the sampled
token $y_t$,
\begin{align}
r_t(\theta)
&:=
\frac{\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta}(y_t\mid h_t)}
     {\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_t\mid h_t)},
\label{eq:def_r}
\\[2pt]
k_t
&:=
\frac{\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_t\mid h_t)}
     {\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_t\mid h_t)},
\label{eq:def_k}
\\[2pt]
p_t
&:=
\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_t\mid h_t).
\label{eq:def_p}
\end{align}
The PPO ratio $r_t(\theta)$ compares two parameter vectors under the
\emph{same} engine; it is a differentiable function of $\theta$, equal to one
at the start of each optimization phase. The mismatch ratio $k_t$ compares the
\emph{same} parameter vector under two engines; it involves no $\theta$, is
fixed once the rollout is scored, and enters the computation graph as a
constant. The confidence $p_t$ is the training-side probability of the token
that was actually sampled.

\subsection{The full correction and its factorization}

The optimizer targets gradients under the current policy as realized by the
training engine, while samples are drawn from
$\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$. The exact importance weight
factorizes as
\begin{equation}
k^{\mathrm{full}}_t(\theta)
:=
\frac{\pi^{\mathrm{trn}}_{\theta}(y_t\mid h_t)}
     {\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_t\mid h_t)}
=
r_t(\theta)\cdot k_t .
\label{eq:telescoping}
\end{equation}
Identity~\eqref{eq:telescoping} induces a division of labor that all methods
compared in this paper share. The parameter-drift factor $r_t(\theta)$ is
consumed by the PPO surrogate,
\begin{equation}
\ell_t(\theta)
=
\min\!\Big(
r_t(\theta)\,\hat A_t,\;
\operatorname{clip}\big(r_t(\theta),\,1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}},\,1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}}\big)\,\hat A_t
\Big),
\label{eq:ppo_surrogate}
\end{equation}
where it serves as a trust-region device rather than as an importance weight.
The engine-mismatch factor $k_t$ is the object this paper studies: it is not
differentiable, not handled by the clip, and---because the inference engine is
never exposed to the current $\theta$---cannot be folded into
\eqref{eq:ppo_surrogate} without destroying the trust-region semantics.
Correcting it is a separate design problem.

\subsection{Ratio operators}

\begin{definition}[Ratio operator]
\label{def:operator}
A \emph{ratio operator} is a measurable map
$M:\R_{+}\times[0,1]\to\R_{+}$ applied tokenwise to the corrected loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{M}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
M(k_t,p_t)\;\ell_t(\theta).
\label{eq:corrected_loss}
\end{equation}
The weight $M(k_t,p_t)$ is a constant with respect to $\theta$ and carries no
gradient. The choice $M(k,p)=k$ recovers exact per-token importance
correction; $M\equiv1$ recovers the uncorrected loss.
\end{definition}

All methods we compare---constant truncation, masking schemes, and the
operator proposed here---are instances of Definition~\ref{def:operator}
acting on the \emph{same} pair $(k_t,p_t)$; they differ only in the shape of
$M$. We restrict attention to operators that may attenuate but never amplify:

\begin{definition}[Shrinkage class]
\label{def:shrinkage}
$\displaystyle
\mathcal M_{\downarrow}
:=
\bigl\{\,M:\ 0<M(k,p)\le k\ \ \text{for all }(k,p)\,\bigr\}$,
equivalently $\log M(k_t,p_t)\le\log k_t$.
\end{definition}

Two structural facts motivate the restriction, both independent of any
distributional model. First, the support bound
$\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}\le1$ implies $k_t\ge p_t$: small ratios occur only at
low-confidence tokens, where they are the correct weights of genuinely
off-policy actions, and where the payload is already attenuated by the score
norm (Lemma~\ref{lem:score_norm}). Second, our dose--response ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) shows that any amount of lower-side lifting
degrades held-out accuracy monotonically, with the symmetric two-sided variant
falling below the untrained baseline.

The only structural property of $k_t$ used in the sequel is that it is
calibrated in conditional mean.

\begin{lemma}[Per-step identity]
\label{lem:identity}
For every prefix $h_t$,
\begin{equation}
\E_{y_t\sim\pi^{\mathrm{inf}}_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid h_t)}
\bigl[k_t\,\big|\,h_t\bigr]=1 .
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof is a one-line change of measure and requires no assumption beyond
support inclusion; we verify \eqref{eq:identity} empirically on both
architectures as a pipeline self-check (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
Notably, \eqref{eq:identity} holds regardless of any contamination of $k_t$,
so mean calibration cannot by itself distinguish a clean mismatch from a
corrupted one; this observation motivates the finer decomposition of the next
section.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}

This section develops the estimation theory behind the operator. The
organizing principle is a distinction between two components of the observed
mismatch that differ not in magnitude but in \emph{epistemic status}: one
component follows a measurable law and is therefore amenable to averaging;
the other is identified only at the level of sets---sign, sparsity,
envelope---and the only well-posed operation against it is a worst-case one.
The resulting objective is a robust mean-squared error, and its minimizer over
the shrinkage class is precisely the deployed operator.

\subsection{Two-channel structure of the mismatch}
\label{sec:two_channel}

Our measurements (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) establish two facts about
$\log k_t$. First, the bulk obeys a \emph{scale law}: the conditional width is
linear in the training-side uncertainty,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{scale}\bigl(\log k_t\,\big|\,p_t\bigr)
=
c\,(1-p_t),
\label{eq:scale_law}
\end{equation}
measured across six decades with $R^2\approx0.999$, and the standardized
deviation collapses to a single pivotal law across confidence bins. Second,
the scale law is \emph{violated} by a sparse set of tokens whose deviations do
not shrink with $1-p_t$: expert-routing discontinuities, whose magnitude is
set by logit gaps rather than by the local noise scale; the finite resolution
of stored log-probabilities; and, in asynchronous deployments, version
displacement between the scoring and generation snapshots, measured at a
median magnitude of $0.12$ that is constant across confidence bins.

These facts fix the model. Write $\varphi_t:=1-p_t$ for now (the
finite-precision floor is introduced in Section~\ref{sec:deployed}).

\begin{assumption}[Scale channel]
\label{asm:scale}
On the scale component,
$\log k^{(0)}_t=c\,\varphi_t\,Z_t$ with $Z_t\mid p_t\sim F_Z$ independent of
$p_t$, $\E[Z_t]=0$, $\E[Z_t^2]<\infty$, $\E[(Z_t)_+]>0$, and
$\E[(Z_t)_-]<\infty$. The upper tail of $F_Z$ is generalized Pareto with
shape $\xi_+\in(0,1)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Residual channel]
\label{asm:residual}
The observed mismatch is
\begin{equation}
\log k_t
=
c\,\varphi_t\,Z_t
+
\delta_t\,B_t,
\label{eq:two_channel}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_t\in\{0,1\}$ with $\Pr(\delta_t=1)=\alpha\le\epsilon$ and
$\delta_t\perp(Z_t,\varphi_t,\ell_t')$, and, conditionally on $\delta_t=1$,
the magnitude satisfies $0\le B_t\le B_{\max}$ with $B_{\max}$ independent of
$\varphi_t$. The magnitude $B_t$ may depend measurably on $Z_t$; independence
is assumed for the indicator only.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:residual} deliberately refrains from modeling the law of
$B_t$. Its three set-level clauses are each measured: sparsity from the
concentration of the standardized distribution outside a thin tail; the
non-scaling envelope from the constancy of residual magnitudes across
confidence bins; one-sidedness of the \emph{threat} from the support bound of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} together with the dose--response ablation, which
jointly confine the harmful direction to the upper side. The per-token value
of $B_t$, by contrast, is unavailable in principle at operator-application
time, and unstable across serving configurations; we therefore treat it as an
adversarial quantity within its measured envelope, in the tradition of
contamination-robust statistics.

The single structural fact driving all results is the
\emph{non-commensurability} of the two channels: in standardized units the
residual displacement is
\begin{equation}
\beta(\varphi)
:=
\frac{B_{\max}}{c\,\varphi}
\;\xrightarrow[\varphi\to0]{}\;\infty,
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
so the same physical displacement that hides within $1.5$ standard deviations
at $p=0.5$ becomes a $10^{2}$-standard-deviation outlier at $p=0.995$.
Standardization is not a cosmetic change of coordinates; it is the mechanism
that separates the channels.

\subsection{The robust estimation objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Let $\ell_t'=\hat A_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid h_t)$ denote the
per-token payload and
$G_0=\E\bigl[e^{c\varphi_tZ_t}\,\ell_t'\bigr]$ the gradient induced by the
scale channel alone---the estimand: contamination is to be removed, not
reproduced. For $M\in\mathcal M_{\downarrow}$, write
$\widehat G_M=n^{-1}\sum_{i}M(k_i,p_i)\ell_i'$. A standard bias--variance
decomposition bounds the mean-squared error by
$\|\E[M\ell']-G_0\|^2+n^{-1}\E[M^2\|\ell'\|^2]$, and we define the
\emph{robust risk} by taking the worst case over the residual mechanism while
averaging over the scale channel:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\mathcal E(M)
:=
\sup_{\{B_t\}\in\,\mathcal U_{\epsilon,B_{\max}}}
\left\{
\Bigl\|\E\bigl[M\,\ell'\bigr]-G_0\Bigr\|^{2}
+
\frac1n\,\E\bigl[M^{2}\|\ell'\|^{2}\bigr]
\right\}.
\;}
\label{eq:robust_risk}
\end{equation}
The asymmetric treatment---expectation over $Z$, supremum over $B$---is not a
preference but a consequence of the knowledge states established in
Section~\ref{sec:two_channel}: a quantity with an identified law admits an
expectation; a quantity known only through a set does not, and the worst case
is the only well-posed alternative. Criteria that average over both channels
are structurally blind to an $O(\epsilon)$ contamination, a point we return
to in Remark~\ref{rem:eps0}.

Two reductions make \eqref{eq:robust_risk} tractable; both are proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof}. First, the score norm obeys
\begin{equation}
(1-p_t)\ \le\ \bigl\|\nabla_z\log\pi(y_t\mid h_t)\bigr\|_2\ \le\ \sqrt2\,(1-p_t)
\label{eq:score_norm}
\end{equation}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:score_norm}), so within a confidence layer the payload norm
factors out of both terms of \eqref{eq:robust_risk} and the layerwise problem
is scalar. Second, parameterizing the operator in standardized coordinates,
$\log M=c\varphi\,\psi(U)$ with $U=\log k/(c\varphi)$, the shrinkage
constraint becomes $\psi(u)\le u$, and the layer risk depends on $\psi$ only
through the truncation deficit $C(\psi)=\E[Z-\psi(Z)]$, the envelope
$\Sigma(\psi)=\sup_u\psi(u)$, and the second moment $\E[\psi(Z)^2]$.

\subsection{Main result}
\label{sec:main_result}

Write $H(\tau):=\E[(Z-\tau)_+]$ for the mean excess of the pivotal law and
$\bar F_Z:=1-F_Z$.

\begin{theorem}[High-confidence minimax operator]
\label{thm:main}
Let the confidence layer satisfy
$\varphi<\varphi^{\ast}:=B_{\max}/(c\,\tau^{\ast})$, so that
$\beta(\varphi)\gg\tau^\ast$ and the reachability defect of
Lemma~\ref{lem:eta_reachability} vanishes. Then, to leading order in
$\epsilon$ and $c\varphi$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\emph{(i)}] the inner supremum in \eqref{eq:robust_risk} is attained by
displacing every contaminated token to the envelope of the operator, and the
worst-case squared bias depends on $\psi$ only through
$\max\{C^2,\ [\epsilon\Sigma-(1-\epsilon)C]^2\}$;
\item[\emph{(ii)}] there is no loss in restricting to the clipping family
$\psi_\tau(z)=\min(z,\tau)$, which attains the minimal feasible envelope at
every truncation deficit, and which the strictly convex $O(1/n)$ second-moment
term selects uniquely;
\item[\emph{(iii)}] the minimizer of $\mathcal E$ over
$\mathcal M_{\downarrow}$ is
\begin{equation}
\log M^{\star}(k_t,p_t)
=
\min\bigl(\log k_t,\ c\,\varphi_t\,\tau^{\ast}\bigr),
\label{eq:optimal_operator}
\end{equation}
where $\tau^{\ast}$ is the unique root of the \emph{equalizer equation}
\begin{equation}
\frac{H(\tau^{\ast})}{\tau^{\ast}}
=
\frac{\epsilon}{2-\epsilon}\,;
\label{eq:equalizer}
\end{equation}
\item[\emph{(iv)}] the finite-sample optimum satisfies
$\tau^\star_n=\tau^\star+O(n^{-1})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}) proceeds in three steps: a
\emph{reachability collapse}---because $\beta(\varphi)\to\infty$, the
adversary can transport any contaminated token to the operator's envelope, so
the worst case is an explicit function of $\Sigma$ rather than of the full
shape of $\psi$; a \emph{minimal-envelope argument}---among all shrinkage
operators with a given truncation deficit, the clip uniquely minimizes the
envelope; and an \emph{equalizer step}---the worst-case bias is the maximum of
a decreasing branch (over-truncation of clean mass) and an increasing branch
(residual leakage), so the optimum sits where the two branches cross, which is
\eqref{eq:equalizer}. Pivotality of $F_Z$ is what makes $\tau^\ast$ a single
number rather than a function of $p$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:equalizer} admits a one-line reading: the operator
deliberately truncates a $\Theta(\epsilon)$ quantile of \emph{clean} mass so
that, at the worst case, the bias of over-truncation exactly balances the
bias of contamination leakage. Clipping here is not variance control; it is
bias equalization against an adversary whose displacement is, at high
confidence, two orders of magnitude larger than the local noise scale.

\begin{corollary}[Compilation of the band]
\label{cor:compile}
Under the generalized Pareto tail of Assumption~\ref{asm:scale},
$H(\tau)\sim\frac{\xi_+}{1-\xi_+}\,\tau\,\bar F_Z(\tau)$, and
\eqref{eq:equalizer} compiles to
\begin{equation}
\bar F_Z\bigl(\tau^{\ast}\bigr)
\;\sim\;
\frac{1-\xi_+}{\xi_+}\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{2-\epsilon},
\qquad
\lambda_+:=c\,\tau^{\ast}.
\label{eq:compile}
\end{equation}
Every quantity on the right is measurable without a training run: $c$ and
$\xi_+$ from the static mismatch measurement, $\epsilon$ from the residual
exceedance rate. The compiled slope carries two falsifiable predictions:
across serving configurations the same $\epsilon$ must move $\lambda_+$
proportionally to $c$, and on a single configuration the empirical exceedance
$\Pr(Z>\lambda_+/c)$ must agree with the independently estimated
contamination rate to within its order of magnitude.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The deployed operator}
\label{sec:deployed}

Assumption~\ref{asm:scale} is measured on the bulk of the confidence range.
As $p_t\to1$ the observed width stops following $c(1-p_t)$ and saturates at
the storage resolution of the log-probabilities---itself an instance of the
residual channel. Below that point the unfloored band would be narrower than
the numerical resolution and the operator would truncate quantization noise
rather than mismatch. We therefore replace the scale variable by its floored
version and deploy
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\log M^{\star}(k_t,p_t)
=
\min\Bigl(
\log k_t,\ \
\lambda_+\,\max\bigl(1-p_t,\ \varepsilon_0\bigr)
\Bigr),
\;}
\label{eq:deployed}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_0$ the measured quantization scale. By
\eqref{eq:score_norm} the tokens the floor spares carry the smallest payload
norms, so the relaxation is inexpensive as well as necessary; the ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} shows that without the floor the band collapses
at high confidence and training diverges.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:theory_discussion}

\begin{remark}[The $\epsilon=0$ section]
\label{rem:eps0}
Setting $\epsilon=0$ removes the only confidence-differentiated force from
\eqref{eq:robust_risk}; the optimum reverts to a constant cap whose threshold
is set by the sample size, $\tau^\star_{\mathrm{var}}=(n-1)\,C(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{var}})$
(Appendix~\ref{app:proof}). Our four independent null measurements---the flat
$\Delta(\varphi)$ surface, the vanishing coupling channel, the deconfounded
bare-sum slope, and the best-response fixed point converging to
$\lambda^\star\approx0$---are therefore not failures of the theory but its
predictions on the $\epsilon=0$ section: each of those probes evaluates a
criterion in which the residual channel is absent by construction. The band
is a strict consequence of $\epsilon>0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Amplitude versus location]
\label{rem:amplitude}
The framework separates two events that every weight-magnitude criterion
conflates. A large $|Z_t|$ is an \emph{amplitude} extreme of the clean
law---a legitimate sample, expensive in variance, correctly handled by the
loose sample-size-driven cap that average-case criteria already produce. A
nonzero $B_t$ is a \emph{location} error: the weight is computed against the
wrong measure. Average-case criteria are blind to it at $O(\epsilon)$; the
robust risk sees it because the supremum is taken before the expectation. The
band is aimed at the location error, and standardization is what makes that
error visible.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Engineering projection]
\label{rem:projection}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is stated for $\varphi<\varphi^{\ast}\approx
B_{\max}/(c\tau^\ast)$. Outside this regime the two populations overlap
($\beta\lesssim\tau^\ast$) and any debiasing depends sensitively on the exact
residual envelope; we make no optimality claim there. The deployed operator
applies the high-confidence slope globally, which is conservative and, in
practice, immaterial: with slope $\lambda_+$ the band triggers only at
$Z_t>\tau^\ast$, and at low confidence typical deviations are $O(1)$ standard
units, so the operator is essentially inactive precisely where the theorem is
silent.
\end{remark}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments answer four questions. \textbf{RQ1}: do logged rollouts support the two-channel model of Section~\ref{sec:two_channel}, and do the measured constants reproduce the predictions of Section~\ref{sec:theory}? \textbf{RQ2}: inside one RL system and under one recipe, does the deployed operator improve held-out accuracy over the cap, the gate, and the exact ratio? \textbf{RQ3}: which elements of the operator matter, and in which direction? \textbf{RQ4}: does the compiled slope transfer across serving configurations as Corollary~\ref{cor:compile} prescribes, and what does the band act on during training? Section~\ref{sec:experiments-setup} describes the shared setup; each subsequent subsection answers one question. Implementation details and additional tables and figures are in Appendix~\ref{app:experimental}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:experiments-setup}

\paragraph{Mismatch measurement.}
We measure the mismatch on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat, a model with 24 sparse layers, 60 experts, and top-4 routing, and on Qwen1.5-14B-Chat as a dense control. For 2{,}500 prompts from DAPO-Math-17k we sample eight responses each with vLLM at temperature one and without truncation, record the log-probability of every sampled token from the sampling step itself, and rescore the same sequences with the training engine at the same parameters. This gives 12.3M tokens for the MoE model and 12.4M for the dense model, each with the token-level pair $(k_t,p_t)$ and, for the MoE model, the expert routes selected on both sides. Two controls fix the measurement floor: rescoring a sequence twice on the training engine returns exactly zero difference, and the empirical mean of $k_t$ is $1.0000$ on both architectures, which verifies Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}.

\paragraph{Training.}
We train with the official AReaL implementation on the 7{,}473 training problems of GSM8K and evaluate on its 1{,}319 held-out test problems by greedy decoding and exact match of the final answer, so that a single arm has a standard error of about $1.3$ points. The policy is Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat, the model on which the mismatch was measured. Rollouts are generated by SGLang in bf16 and gradients are computed by FSDP in bf16 with flash attention. Every arm uses the same recipe: learning rate $3\times10^{-6}$, weight decay $0.01$, PPO clip range $(0.2,0.28)$, no KL penalty, four samples per prompt at temperature one, at most 1{,}024 generated tokens, at most two versions of rollout staleness, three epochs (87 optimizer steps), and eight A100 GPUs per arm. The baselines are instances of Definition~\ref{def:operator} in the same code path: no correction ($M\equiv1$), the exact ratio ($M=k$), TIS with cap $2$, IcePop with mask $[0.5,5]$, and the KL-based masking variant of AReaL, which we label KPop, with threshold $2$. Our operator is~\eqref{eq:deployed} with $\lambda_+=2.3$ and $\varepsilon_0=5\times10^{-3}$. Each method is run with three seeds. For the serving-configuration test we additionally run every method with the SGLang KV cache quantized to FP8, which enlarges the mismatch. Held-out accuracy at the final checkpoint is the arbiter of every comparison; training reward is reported but is not used to choose between methods.

\subsection{RQ1: Do rollout logs support the two-channel model?}
\label{sec:experiments-diagnostics}

\paragraph{Scale channel.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows the conditional distribution of $\log k_t$. Its width follows the linear law~\eqref{eq:scale_law} across six decades of uncertainty, with $c=0.155$ for the MoE model and $c=0.035$ for the dense model and a weighted $R^2$ of $0.998$ over twenty equal-mass bins. After standardization by $c\varphi_t$ the central quantiles of $Z_t$ collapse across bins: between $\varphi=5\times10^{-3}$ and $\varphi=1$ the median moves by $0.06$ and the two deciles by $0.35$ and $1.17$ (Figure~\ref{fig:pivot}a). The upper tail is generalized Pareto with $\xi_+=0.49$ above the 99th percentile for the MoE model and $\xi_+=0.07$ for the dense model, so the MoE tail is heavy and some truncation is necessary rather than optional.

\paragraph{Residual channel.}
We call a token residual when $|Z_t|>\kappa$ with the preregistered $\kappa=5$. Residual tokens are sparse, with $\hat\epsilon=1.26\%$ ($2.16\%$ at $\kappa=4$ and $0.78\%$ at $\kappa=6$), and their indicator is uncorrelated with $\varphi_t$ (correlation $0.004$). They cluster only weakly within trajectories: the lag-one autocorrelation of the indicator is $0.041$ against a permutation null of $0.0002$, and the conditional lift decays from $4.2$ at lag one to $2.6$ at lag four. At high confidence ($\varphi<0.05$) the residual displacement is one-sided, with $97.7\%$ of residual tokens positive on the base model and $100\%$ on the trained policy, and it is bounded, with a 99th percentile of $0.33$. Residual tokens are enriched in hard routing flips, which we define as a route change with gate margin above $10^{-3}$, by a factor of $1.46$ over the base rate of $0.53$, and they carry $2.0$ flipped layers on average against $1.1$ overall; the enrichment is real but not decisive, so routing is one contributor to the residual channel rather than its only cause. On the base model the residual median magnitude still scales with $\varphi$, at a fixed factor of about six above the bulk (Figure~\ref{fig:dualscale}a). The non-scaling behavior of Assumption~\ref{asm:residual} appears once the policy has been trained and once the two engines are displaced in version. On the same tokens scored under a snapshot that is 29 steps stale, and in the logs of the training run itself, the residual magnitude at $\varphi<0.02$ lies between $0.04$ and $0.11$ while $c\varphi$ lies one to two orders of magnitude below it (Figure~\ref{fig:dualscale}b,c). We therefore state the non-scaling envelope as a high-confidence property, which is the regime in which Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is invoked. The paired measurement also fixes the sign structure of version displacement: rescoring the same tokens under the same snapshot through the two decode paths gives exactly zero difference, the displacement between snapshots is two-sided overall ($48.9\%$ positive) with a negative median at low confidence which reflects policy sharpening, and it is entirely positive on the high-confidence residual tokens that the operator acts on.

\paragraph{Compilation and its predictions.}
With $\hat\epsilon=1.76\%$ measured off the quantization floor and the empirical $F_Z$, the equalizer equation~\eqref{eq:equalizer} has the root $\tau^\star=5.7$, which lies at the knee where the standardized bulk turns into the Pareto tail (Figure~\ref{fig:pivot}b,c). At the base-model scale this compiles to $\lambda_+=c\tau^\star=0.9$. The scale constant grows during training, from $0.16$ on the base model to $0.61$ in the first third of the run and $1.67$ in the last third, so the same $\tau^\star$ compiles to $3.5$ at the early-training scale. The deployed slope $\lambda_+=2.3$ lies between these two values, and the sweep of Section~\ref{sec:experiments-analysis} places the held-out optimum on the plateau $\lambda_+\in\{1.6,2.3\}$. Corollary~\ref{cor:compile} makes two falsifiable statements. First, the exceedance $\Pr(Z>\lambda_+/c)$ should agree with an independently estimated contamination rate within an order of magnitude; we measure $0.13\%$ against a version-staleness rate of $0.5$ to $0.6\%$ in the training logs. Second, a change of serving configuration should move the required slope in proportion to $c$; Section~\ref{sec:experiments-transfer} tests this directly.

\paragraph{Null measurements on the $\epsilon=0$ section.}
Remark~\ref{rem:eps0} predicts that criteria which average over both channels do not produce the band. Four independent measurements on the same logs agree with this prediction. The sequence-level surface $\Delta(\varphi)$ has slope $-0.002$ with a trajectory-bootstrap interval of $[-0.48,0.15]$ over $20{,}000$ trajectories; the coupling coefficient $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=c(\beta_1-\tfrac32\beta_2)$ has an interval which covers zero in three independent batches; the bare leave-one-out sum has slope $0.31$ against $\varphi$, of which $0.25$ survives a permutation that destroys within-trajectory coupling; and best-response iteration of the sequence-level risk converges from every start to a constant cap with $\lambda^\star=0.07$. The coherence check for Assumption~(A3) gives a far-layer gradient coherence of $0.10$ between confidence layers, which is indistinguishable from its permutation null, and within a layer the token gradient directions are nearly orthogonal ($\rho\approx0.005$). RQ1 is therefore answered in the affirmative for the scale channel, for sparsity, sign, and boundedness of the residual channel, and for both compiled predictions, with the qualification that the non-scaling envelope is a high-confidence property.

\paragraph{One-sidedness and dose response.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} varies one element of the operator at a time. Lifting the lower side degrades held-out accuracy monotonically with the amount of lifting: from $64.67$ with no lower bound to $63.15$ at $w\ge0.001$, to $56.10$ at $w\ge0.1$, and to $48.98$ for the symmetric band, which is below the untrained model. The training reward does not register this harm; the $w\ge0.1$ variant reaches the highest training reward of the entire study while its held-out accuracy returns to the uncorrected level. An intermediate checkpoint of that run scores $62.62$ after one epoch and $56.10$ after three, so the harm accumulates as the policy sharpens and more tokens fall into the lifted region. Together with the support bound $k_t\ge p_t$, this is the empirical basis for restricting to the shrinkage class of Definition~\ref{def:shrinkage}.

\paragraph{Floor, band shape, and slope.}
Without a floor the band collapses at high confidence: in the third epoch $32\%$ of tokens are truncated, the truncated tokens have median $|\log k_t|=7\times10^{-4}$, which is the resolution of the stored log-probabilities, and training diverges at step 79. With $\varepsilon_0=5\times10^{-3}$ the truncated tokens have median $|\log k_t|$ between $0.08$ and $0.12$. Raising the floor to $\varepsilon_0=0.1$, which places $58\%$ of tokens under a constant cap of $0.23$, costs $3.3$ points on three seeds while leaving the training reward unchanged; the floor is the storage resolution, not the residual scale. Adding a constant $0.12$ to the band costs $1.8$ points, which agrees with the null measurements of Section~\ref{sec:experiments-diagnostics}: the data support a multiplicative band and do not support additive widening. The slope is robust on the plateau $\lambda_+\in\{1.6,2.3\}$ and declines slightly at $3.1$. RQ3 is answered as follows: the lower side must be left open, the floor must sit at the storage resolution, the band must scale multiplicatively with $\varphi_t$, and the slope is insensitive within a factor of two.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Token-level ratio operators encode assumptions about an observation-law class. The bounded-band theorem gives capping both a population origin and an exact validity boundary. The localized-corruption theorem shows that gating is not uniformly justified without localization; once the nuisance support is localized, the exact solution is an identity-plus-clean-tail cap, and IcePop is the sharp zero-cap phase selected by a necessary-and-sufficient mass condition. In both cases, attaining members match the upper guarantees exactly. The remaining empirical question is which class is supported by rollout logs in a given model and system regime.

\section{Standing Assumptions and Notation}
\label{app:standing}
 
Throughout the appendix: $F=F_Z$ with density $f$, standardized so that $\E[Z]=0$;
$\Fbar=1-F$; $H(\tau)=\E[(Z-\tau)_+]=\int_\tau^\infty \Fbar(z)\,dz$;
$\beta=B_{\max}/(c\varphi)$. All statements are within a confidence layer (fixed $p$,
hence fixed $\varphi$), to first order in $c\varphi$ (A4) and in $\epsilon$ (A5).
 
\emph{(A1) Scale family and pivotality.} $\log k^{(0)}_t=c\varphi_tZ_t$ with
$Z_t\sim F_Z$ independent of $p_t$ on the core; upper tail generalized Pareto with shape
$\xi_+\in(0,1)$.
 
\emph{(A2) Contamination.} As in Definition~\ref{def:threat}; in particular
$(Z_t,\delta_t)\perp B_t$ and $\delta_t\perp(\varphi_t,\ell_t')$.
 
\emph{(A3) Cross-layer aggregation.} Either layer gradient directions are mutually
near-orthogonal, or the bias is aggregated by the norm-conservative bound
$\|\sum_p b_p\|\le\sum_p\|b_p\|$; in both cases the layerwise optimum is optimal for the
aggregate. The empirical check is the coherence matrix of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
 
\emph{(A4) Linearization.} $e^{c\varphi u}=1+c\varphi u+O((c\varphi)^2)$; with
$c\varphi\le0.156$ the neglected terms are $\le2.5\%$ relative.
 
\emph{(A5) First order in $\epsilon$.} Terms $O(\epsilon^2)$ are dropped; validity is
guarded by the sparsity and non-clustering measurements.
 
\section{The Score-Norm Lemma}
\label{app:score}
 
\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:score}
Let $\pi=\pold(\cdot\mid h_t)$ and $p_t=\pi_{y_t}$. Then
\begin{equation}
(1-p_t)\ \le\ \big\|\nabla_z\log\pold(y_t\mid h_t)\big\|_2\ \le\ \sqrt2\,(1-p_t).
\label{eq:score_bounds}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
 
\begin{proof}
Differentiating the log-softmax,
\begin{align}
\nabla_z\log\pi_{y_t}
&\eqlab{a}
e_{y_t}-\pi,
\nonumber\\
\big\|e_{y_t}-\pi\big\|_2^{2}
&\eqlab{b}
(1-p_t)^{2}+\sum_{j\ne y_t}\pi_j^{2},
\nonumber\\
\sum_{j\ne y_t}\pi_j^{2}
&\lelab{c}
\Big(\max_{j\ne y_t}\pi_j\Big)\sum_{j\ne y_t}\pi_j
\;\le\;
(1-p_t)^{2},
\label{eq:score_proof}
\end{align}
where (a) is $\partial\log\pi_{y}/\partial z_j=\ind\{j=y\}-\pi_j$; (b) expands the squared
norm; (c) bounds each term by the largest off-target probability, itself at most
$\sum_{j\ne y_t}\pi_j=1-p_t$. The lower bound keeps only the first term of (b). Both ends
are tight: the lower as off-target mass spreads over many tokens, the upper as it
concentrates on one.
\end{proof}
 
For the unembedding block $z=Wh_t$, the identity
$\nabla_W\log\pi_{y_t}=(e_{y_t}-\pi)h_t^{\top}$ gives
$\|\nabla_W\log\pi_{y_t}\|_F=\|e_{y_t}-\pi\|_2\|h_t\|_2$, so \eqref{eq:score_bounds}
transfers verbatim when $\|h_t\|\perp p_t$ (enforced by the final normalization layer and
verified directly). For the full parameter vector only the upper bound survives, via
$\|\nabla_\theta\log\pi\|\le\|J_\theta\|\,\|e_{y_t}-\pi\|$; using the error-vector norm as
a per-example gradient-norm proxy is standard practice. Within a layer we therefore write
$\|\ell_t'\|=\kappa_t(1-p_t)$ with $\kappa_t\perp p_t$ (checks C1--C2 of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
 
\section{The Inner Supremum}
\label{app:worst}
 
\begin{lemma}[Worst-case contamination]
\label{lem:worst}
Fix a clip operator $\psi_\tau(u)=\min(u,\tau)$ in standardized coordinates. Both terms of
\eqref{eq:robust_risk} are nondecreasing in each $B_t$; consequently the supremum over
$\Umodel$ is attained at $B_t\equiv B_{\max}$.
\end{lemma}
 
\begin{proof}
Write the contaminated token's output error against the clean target,
\begin{align}
D(B)
&\eqlab{a}
c\varphi\Big[\min\big(Z+\tfrac{B}{c\varphi},\ \tau\big)-Z\Big],
\nonumber\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\,\min\big(Z+\tfrac{B}{c\varphi},\tau\big)
&\eqlab{b}
\tfrac{1}{c\varphi}\,\ind\big\{Z+\tfrac{B}{c\varphi}<\tau\big\}
\;\ge\;0,
\label{eq:worst_monotone}
\end{align}
where (a) subtracts the clean value $c\varphi Z$ from the clipped observed value; (b)
differentiates the min. Hence $D(B)$ is nondecreasing in $B$, and since $D(0)=0$ while
$B\ge0$, the bias contribution of every contaminated token is maximized at $B=B_{\max}$.
The variance contribution is $\E[m^2\|\ell'\|^2]$ with
$m=e^{c\varphi\min(Z+B/(c\varphi),\tau)}$, also nondecreasing in $B$ by
\eqref{eq:worst_monotone}. A supremum of a sum of terms each maximized at the same point is
attained there.
\end{proof}
 
Two remarks. First, the adversary controls magnitudes only; placement is Bernoulli and
independent by A2, which is the measured non-clustering. Under a placement-adversarial
model the analysis would change (the adversary would park contamination just below
trigger); that model is excluded by measurement, not by convenience. Second, on
$\{Z\le\tau-\beta\}$ the contamination passes through untouched with full residual
$B_{\max}$; in the high-confidence regime $\beta\gg\tau$ this set has clean-law probability
$F(\tau-\beta)\approx0$ (left tail), which is where the regime restriction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} enters.
 
\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}
\label{app:proof}
 
By Lemma~\ref{lem:worst} set $B_t\equiv B_{\max}$, so every contaminated token observes
$Z^{\mathrm{obs}}=Z+\beta$. All computations are per layer; the payload factor
$\kappa^2(1-p)^2$ multiplies both bias$^2$ and variance and is dropped throughout (it is
the exact cancellation that defeats plain MSE; here the $\varphi$-dependence survives
inside the contamination geometry, not in the payload factor).
 
\subsection{Bias decomposition}
 
Write $D_t:=\log M-\log k^{(0)}$ in log-weight units (equal to the weight-scale error to
$O((c\varphi)^2)$ by A4). Under the clip $\psi_\tau$:
\begin{align}
\text{clean }(\delta=0):\qquad
D
&\eqlab{a}
c\varphi\big[\min(Z,\tau)-Z\big]
=
-\,c\varphi\,(Z-\tau)_+ ,
\label{eq:D_clean}
\\[2pt]
\text{contaminated }(\delta=1):\qquad
D
&\eqlab{b}
c\varphi\big[\min(Z+\beta,\tau)-Z\big]
=
\begin{cases}
+\,c\varphi\,\beta, & Z\le\tau-\beta,\\[2pt]
+\,c\varphi\,(\tau-Z), & \tau-\beta<Z\le\tau,\\[2pt]
-\,c\varphi\,(Z-\tau), & Z>\tau,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:D_contam}
\end{align}
where (a) and (b) subtract the clean value $c\varphi Z$; the three cases are full
pass-through, partial removal, and truncation beyond the clean value. Taking expectations
and mixing with weights $(1-\epsilon,\epsilon)$,
\begin{align}
b(\tau)
&\eqlab{a}
(1-\epsilon)\big(-c\varphi H(\tau)\big)
+\epsilon\,c\varphi\Big[\beta F(\tau-\beta)
+\!\int_{\tau-\beta}^{\tau}\!(\tau-z)f(z)\,dz
- H(\tau)\Big]
\nonumber\\
&\eqlab{b}
c\varphi\Big[
-\,H(\tau)
+\epsilon\Big(\beta F(\tau-\beta)+\!\int_{\tau-\beta}^{\tau}\!(\tau-z)f(z)\,dz\Big)
\Big],
\label{eq:bias_formula}
\end{align}
where (a) integrates \eqref{eq:D_clean}--\eqref{eq:D_contam} case by case; (b) collects
$-(1-\epsilon)H-\epsilon H=-H$. The clean and contaminated contributions enter with
opposite signs: over-truncation of clean mass can cancel the contamination residual, and
the optimum will exploit exactly this.
 
\subsection{Monotonicity and the unique root}
 
Differentiate \eqref{eq:bias_formula} term by term:
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{d\tau}\big[-H(\tau)\big]
&\eqlab{a}
\Fbar(\tau),
\nonumber\\
\frac{d}{d\tau}\big[\beta F(\tau-\beta)\big]
&\eqlab{b}
\beta f(\tau-\beta),
\nonumber\\
\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\tau-\beta}^{\tau}(\tau-z)f(z)\,dz
&\eqlab{c}
\underbrace{(\tau-\tau)f(\tau)}_{=0}
-\underbrace{\beta f(\tau-\beta)}_{\text{lower limit}}
+\int_{\tau-\beta}^{\tau}f(z)\,dz,
\label{eq:leibniz}
\end{align}
where (a) is $H'=-\Fbar$; (b) is the chain rule; (c) is Leibniz's rule, whose lower-limit
term cancels (b) \emph{exactly}. Hence
\begin{equation}
b'(\tau)
=
c\varphi\Big[\Fbar(\tau)+\epsilon\big(F(\tau)-F(\tau-\beta)\big)\Big]
\;>\;0 ,
\label{eq:bprime}
\end{equation}
so $b$ is strictly increasing. Its limits:
\begin{align}
b(0^{+})
&\eqlab{a}
c\varphi\big[-H(0)+O(\epsilon)\big]<0,
\qquad
b(\infty)
\eqlab{b}
c\varphi\,\epsilon\,\beta
=\epsilon B_{\max}>0,
\label{eq:blimits}
\end{align}
where (a) uses $H(0)=\E[Z_+]>0$ and, at high confidence, $F(-\beta)\approx0$ so the
$\epsilon$-terms are $O(\epsilon\,\E[Z_-])$; (b) uses $H(\infty)=0$,
$F(\tau-\beta)\to1$, and the vanishing of the window integral. Therefore $b$ has a unique
zero $\tau_{\mathrm{unb}}\in(0,\infty)$: the \emph{debiasing point}, at which deliberate
clean truncation cancels the contamination residual in expectation.
 
\subsection{Second-order verification}
 
The bias term of the risk is $b^2$. Its stationary points satisfy $b\,b'=0$; since
$b'>0$ everywhere by \eqref{eq:bprime}, the only stationary point is
$b(\tau_{\mathrm{unb}})=0$, and there
\begin{equation}
\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\,b^{2}
=
2\big(b'\big)^{2}+2\,b\,b''
\eqlab{a}
2\big(b'(\tau_{\mathrm{unb}})\big)^{2}
>0,
\label{eq:second_order}
\end{equation}
where (a) uses $b(\tau_{\mathrm{unb}})=0$. The debiasing point is the strict global
minimum of the bias term; no other stationary branch exists.
 
\subsection{High-confidence limit}
 
Let $\beta\gg\tau$ over the relevant range (the regime $\varphi<\varphi^\ast$ of the
theorem). Then $F(\tau-\beta)\approx0$ and the window integral extends to the whole left
half:
\begin{align}
\int_{\tau-\beta}^{\tau}(\tau-z)f(z)\,dz
&\eqlab{a}
\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}(\tau-z)f(z)\,dz+O\big(F(\tau-\beta)\big)
\nonumber\\
&\eqlab{b}
\tau F(\tau)-\E\big[Z\big]+\E\big[Z\ind\{Z>\tau\}\big]+O(\cdot)
\nonumber\\
&\eqlab{c}
\tau+H(\tau)+O\big(F(\tau-\beta)\big),
\label{eq:window}
\end{align}
where (a) extends the lower limit; (b) integrates by parts; (c) uses $\E[Z]=0$ and
$\E[Z\ind\{Z>\tau\}]=H(\tau)+\tau\Fbar(\tau)$. Substituting into
\eqref{eq:bias_formula},
\begin{equation}
b(\tau)
=
c\varphi\Big[-(1-\epsilon)\,H(\tau)+\epsilon\,\tau\Big]+O\big(\epsilon F(\tau-\beta)\big),
\qquad
b(\tau^{\ast})=0
\iff
\frac{H(\tau^{\ast})}{\tau^{\ast}}
=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},
\label{eq:debias_proof}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:debias}. Under the generalized Pareto upper tail,
$\Fbar(z)\asymp C z^{-1/\xi_+}$ and
\begin{align}
H(\tau)
&\eqlab{a}
\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\Fbar(z)\,dz
\eqlab{b}
\frac{\xi_+}{1-\xi_+}\,\tau\,\Fbar(\tau)\,\big(1+o(1)\big),
\label{eq:gpd_H}
\end{align}
where (a) is Fubini and (b) integrates the power law. Substituting into
\eqref{eq:debias_proof} gives \eqref{eq:gpd_root}. This proves parts (ii) and (iv).
 
\subsection{Variance term}
 
The variance contribution, after the common factor is removed and linearized by A4, is
$V(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{n}\big[1+2c\varphi\,\E[\min(Z^{\mathrm{obs}},\tau)]\big]+O((c\varphi)^2)$,
with $V'(\tau)>0$: loosening the cap always increases the second moment (and without a
cap it diverges under $\xi_+>0$, which is the variance route to the necessity of
truncation). The full first-order condition is $2b\,b'+V'=0$; at $\tau_{\mathrm{unb}}$
the first term vanishes while $V'>0$, so the full optimum satisfies
$\tau^{\star}_{\mathrm{full}}<\tau_{\mathrm{unb}}$ with
$\tau_{\mathrm{unb}}-\tau^{\star}_{\mathrm{full}}=O(1/n)$ by the implicit function
theorem applied at the strict minimum \eqref{eq:second_order}. This proves part (iii);
part (i) is Lemma~\ref{lem:worst}.
\hfill$\square$
 
\subsection{Outside the regime}
 
For $\varphi>\varphi^{\ast}$ the two populations overlap ($\beta\lesssim\tau$),
$F(\tau-\beta)$ is no longer negligible, and the root of \eqref{eq:bias_formula} depends
on $\beta$, hence on the exact value of $B_{\max}$: a $1.5\sigma$ contamination is
statistically entangled with clean fluctuation, and any debiasing there is sensitive to
misspecification of the contamination magnitude. We therefore make no optimality claim in
this regime. The deployed single-slope operator is inactive there (typical deviations are
$O(1)\sigma$ against a cap of $\tau^\ast\sigma$), so the projection costs nothing where
the theory is silent; the dose--response ablation bounds the cost of the conservative
choice empirically.
 
\section{Why Weight-Error Criteria Do Not Produce the Band}
\label{app:criteria}
 
For contrast we record the optima of the two natural non-robust criteria under the same
measured laws, both derived by the pointwise variation of Section~\ref{sec:seqmse}.
 
\emph{Sequence-level risk.} With
$\mathcal R_n=(\E|W_M-W|)^2+\tfrac1n\E[W_M^2]$ and pivotal $Z$, the surface
$\Delta=\log L_2-\log L_1$ is flat in $\varphi$ and the optimum is the constant cap
$M=\min(k,\bar C)$; this is the analytic counterpart of the four null measurements
(Remark~\ref{rem:eps0}).
 
\emph{Token-level mean-squared error.} With the clean estimand and the exact conditional
first-order condition,
\begin{align}
C(k,p)
&\eqlab{a}
\frac{n\,\langle-b,\ v(k,p)\rangle}{s(k,p)},
\qquad
v=\E[\ell'\mid k,p],\quad s=\E[\norm{\ell'}^2\mid k,p],
\nonumber\\
&\eqlab{b}
\frac{n\,\langle-b,\bar u\rangle}{\kappa}\,(1-p)^{-1},
\label{eq:token_mse}
\end{align}
where (a) is the pointwise stationarity of bias$^2$ plus variance; (b) substitutes
Lemma~\ref{lem:score}: the payload norm enters the numerator once (bias is linear in $m$)
and the denominator twice (variance is quadratic), leaving one inverse power. The cap
\emph{loosens} with confidence---the opposite of the deployed operator. The mechanism is
transparent: high-confidence tokens carry small payload norms, so their weight errors are
cheap in variance, and any average-case criterion spares them. The contamination they
carry is invisible at $O(\epsilon)$ to an average-case criterion and decisive to the
robust one; that reversal, not any expansion order, is the content of
Remark~\ref{rem:amplitude}.

\section{Experimental Details}
\label{app:experimental}

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{app:experimental-diagnostics}

\paragraph{Static measurement.}
Prompts are 2{,}500 problems drawn at random from DAPO-Math-17k. Generation uses vLLM 0.26 in bf16 with eager execution and without prefix caching; each prompt receives eight samples at temperature one and top-$p$ one, at most 1{,}024 tokens per response, and the log-probability of every sampled token is taken from the sampling step itself rather than from a later prefill pass. Rescoring uses HuggingFace Transformers in bf16 with teacher forcing on the exact sampled sequence and the same parameter snapshot; the token at position $t$ is scored from the logits at position $t-1$ on both sides. For the MoE model we record the top-4 expert identities of every layer on both sides and the gate margin between the fourth and fifth expert; the routing read from forward hooks agrees with the identities returned by the fused kernel on $100\%$ of the (token, layer) pairs whose margin is positive. Three controls accompany the main measurement: rescoring in fp32, rescoring the sequence twice on the training engine (zero difference), and rescoring by a prefill pass on the inference engine, which reproduces the decode-time bulk. Bins are equal-mass in $p_t$; scale is the median absolute deviation times $1.4826$; the constant $c$ is a weighted least-squares fit of the bin scale to the bin median of $1-p_t$ on the bins whose median $1-p_t$ exceeds $10^{-3}$; residual bins with fewer than fifty tokens are excluded; confidence intervals are trajectory bootstraps with 500 resamples over 4{,}000 trajectories.

\paragraph{Paired version displacement.}
To observe the displacement of $\log k_t$ under version staleness on the same tokens, we take three consecutive epoch checkpoints of the deployed-operator run (steps 28, 57, and 86), generate 4{,}000 GSM8K rollouts with the step-57 snapshot as the rollout engine, and rescore every generated token under the step-86 snapshot on the training engine and under the step-28, step-57, and step-86 snapshots on the inference engine by prefill. The difference between the two decode paths of the same snapshot is exactly zero, so the difference between snapshots is the version displacement alone. On these 508{,}915 tokens the displacement is two-sided overall, its median at low confidence is negative because the newer policy has moved probability away from tokens which the older policy sampled with low confidence, its 99th percentile is $0.68$ at a lag of 29 steps and $2.93$ at a lag of 58 steps, and among high-confidence residual tokens it is entirely positive. On tokens generated and scored at the same parameter version, the scale constant of the trained policy is $0.52$, three times its value on the base model.

\paragraph{Training-time logging.}
The training runs dump, every ten optimizer calls, the rollout-side log-probability, the training-side log-probability at the start of the update, and the parameter version of the rollout for a sample of tokens. Version staleness is the difference between the current version and the rollout version. The training-time scale constant is fitted on the tokens with zero staleness in each third of the run. The trigger statistics of Figure~\ref{fig:trigger} are computed from these dumps; the IcePop population in panel (b) is the set of tokens whose ratio leaves $[0.5,5]$ in the IcePop run.

\paragraph{Sequence-level null measurements.}
The four measurements of Remark~\ref{rem:eps0} are computed on the static logs with the deployed operator applied at $\lambda_+=2.3$. The surface $\Delta(\varphi)=\log\E[e^{S_{-t}}\mid\varphi]-\log\E[e^{2S_{-t}}\mid\varphi]$, where $S_{-t}$ is the leave-one-out sum of $\log M$ over the trajectory, is estimated with log-sum-exp in twenty geometric bins of $\varphi$; its slope is fitted by weighted least squares and bootstrapped over trajectories. The coefficients $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ are the slopes of the conditional mean and the conditional variance of $S_{-t}$ against $c\varphi$. The bare-sum slope is the coefficient of $\varphi$ in a regression of the leave-one-out sum of $\log k$; its permutation null shuffles $\log k$ across tokens while keeping the length of every trajectory. The best-response iteration solves the sequence-level risk for the two-parameter family $\min\{k,Ce^{\lambda(1-p)}\}$ from five initial points. All four are reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments-diagnostics}.

\subsection{Implementation and Hardware}
\label{app:experimental-implementation}

Training uses AReaL v2.0.0 with its decoupled loss, in which the rollout-side and training-side log-probabilities are both available per token. All operators are implemented in the same token-level branch that AReaL uses for TIS and IcePop, so that every arm differs only in the map $M$; the deployed operator multiplies the loss by $\exp\big(\min(\log k_t,\lambda_+\max(1-p_t,\varepsilon_0))\big)$ with $p_t$ taken from the training-side log-probability, and it carries no gradient. Three changes to the framework were needed for a shared cluster: three hard-coded worker-readiness timeouts were raised from 30 to 300 seconds, a binary-KL option was added to the mask whitelist, and a token-level dump hook was added. The training engine must use flash attention; with packed sequences the scaled-dot-product fallback attends across sequence boundaries and corrupts the training-side log-probabilities, which we detected as a median $\log k_t$ far from zero on every method. Each arm runs on one node with eight A100-80GB GPUs, with four GPUs serving SGLang (data parallel) and four training with FSDP, and takes between $1.3$ and $3.5$ hours depending on node sharing. Evaluation uses vLLM with tensor parallelism of four, greedy decoding, at most 2{,}048 tokens, and exact match of the last number in the response against the GSM8K answer. The FP8 runs set the SGLang KV-cache dtype to \texttt{fp8\_e5m2} and change nothing else.

\subsection{Preregistered Evaluation Contract}
\label{app:experimental-contract}

The following choices were fixed before the corresponding runs. The residual threshold is $\kappa=5$, with $\kappa\in\{4,6\}$ reported as sensitivity. The floor is $\varepsilon_0=5\times10^{-3}$, set from the storage resolution of the log-probabilities before any training run. The slope grid is $\lambda_+\in\{1.6,2.3,3.1\}$ with $\lambda_-\in\{1.0,\infty\}$. All arms use the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:experiments-setup} without per-arm tuning. Held-out accuracy at the final checkpoint is the arbiter of every comparison; training reward is reported but is not used to select between methods. Every ablation changes one element of the operator relative to the deployed configuration. Results marked as seed one are single runs and are read against a standard error of $1.3$ points; the main comparison and the floor ablation use three seeds. The dose--response ablation, the affine variant, the raised floor, and the FP8 rescaling were added after the main comparison; for each of them the value to be tested was fixed from a measurement before the run started, and the outcome was recorded regardless of direction.

\section{Additional Results and Scope}
\label{app:discussion}

\paragraph{Uniform bias rather than finite-sample MSE.}
\Cref{thm:band-capping,thm:localized-gating,prop:posterior-gate} optimize a uniform bias constant or its integrated analogue. Sampling variance is not part of the population criterion except for the calibration bound in \Cref{thm:band-capping}. Adding finite-sample MSE generally creates an interior bias--variance tradeoff and requires second moments, sample size, and a covariance model.

\paragraph{Localization is a theorem condition.}
The statistical separation between capping and zeroing comes from restricting the corrupted ratio support to $\Lset$. Payload-validity diagnostics must test whether corrupted tokens are concentrated in the declared region. If localization fails, \Cref{eq:nonidentifiability-gap} is the safe comparison and zeroing has no class-wide advantage over the cap at the same upper level.

\paragraph{The escape set is estimated in practice.}
\Cref{thm:localized-gating} conditions on a fixed $\Lset$. Replacing it by $\widehat\Lset$ adds clean fidelity error bounded by
\begin{equation}
B\E_{P_0}[K\1\{K\in\Lset\triangle\widehat\Lset\}],
\end{equation}
while the true reachable-set supremum may change discontinuously unless physical reachability near the estimated boundary is controlled. The calibration rule should therefore be frozen and its boundary sensitivity reported separately.

\paragraph{True supremum versus essential supremum.}
The localized class permits a corrupted law to concentrate on a physically reachable ratio that has zero clean probability. This is why \Cref{eq:localized-risk} uses a true supremum over $\Lset$, not a $P_0$-essential supremum. The fixed-density model in \Cref{eq:density-risk} instead integrates with respect to $g$ and is insensitive to $g$-null points.

\paragraph{Channel separability.}
The matching constructions in \Cref{lem:joint-risk} independently choose clean and corrupted conditional payload means and align them along a common vector. This is appropriate for the latent-channel class in \Cref{eq:localized-class}. If a physical constraint forces both channels to share the same payload map at every overlapping ratio, \Cref{eq:localized-risk} remains an upper bound, but exact equality may fail.

\paragraph{Unknown side allocation.}
\Cref{cor:two-sided} assumes fixed masses $\eps_-$ and $\eps_+$. If only total mass $\eps$ is fixed and its side allocation is unspecified, the exposure term becomes
\begin{equation}
\eps B\max\left\{\sup_{k\in\Lset_-}\abs{M(k)},\sup_{k\in\Lset_+}\abs{M(k)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The side problems no longer separate. The fixed-mass formulation should therefore be used only when side-specific route-disagreement rates are stable enough to support it.
